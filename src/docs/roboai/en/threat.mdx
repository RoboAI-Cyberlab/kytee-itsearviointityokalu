Cyber threats refer to potential malicious activities or events that can harm an organization's information systems or data. Vulnerabilities, on the other hand, are weaknesses in systems, software, or processes that can be exploited by threat actors. Together, they form the basis of cybersecurity risk management, where an organization seeks to identify and reduce potential threats and vulnerabilities.

**Objectives:**

1. **Vulnerability reduction**: The organization aims to identify, assess, and address vulnerabilities in its systems and processes that could endanger the operation or security of critical functions. Vulnerability management includes continuous monitoring, assessment of potential vulnerabilities, and systematic measures to mitigate them.

2. **Threat defense and information sharing**: The organization aims to identify cyber threats that may target its operations and develop defense mechanisms to prevent or respond to these threats. Threat defense also includes active information sharing, both within the organization and with external partners, such as authorities or industry networks, to respond to evolving threats in a coordinated manner.

These objectives help the organization build a comprehensive picture of the threats and vulnerabilities affecting its operations, and enable it to respond quickly and effectively to changing threat landscapes. By improving threat and vulnerability management, the organization can ensure better cybersecurity and protection of critical functions.

<ObjectiveTitle name="threat-1">Vulnerability reduction</ObjectiveTitle>

### Significance

This objective focuses on systematically identifying and addressing vulnerabilities in the organization's information systems. **Vulnerabilities** are weaknesses that can be exploited by threat actors to gain unauthorized access, disrupt operations, or cause other damage. Identifying and addressing vulnerabilities helps reduce the likelihood of successful cyber attacks and minimizes their impacts.

The organization aims to actively identify and assess vulnerabilities, for example through vulnerability scanning and security assessments, and develop measures to address them. This includes both technical aspects, such as software updates, and organizational measures, such as improving processes and training.

### Answer instructions

This objective assesses how effectively the organization identifies, evaluates, and addresses vulnerabilities in its information systems and processes. The objective is to reduce risks arising from vulnerabilities through systematic monitoring and corrective actions.

When evaluating this objective, consider:

- How systematically does the organization identify vulnerabilities in its systems and software?
- Are vulnerability information sources, such as software vendor bulletins or security community reports, used regularly?
- Are vulnerability assessments performed, and how systematically and regularly are they conducted?
- How quickly and effectively does the organization respond to identified vulnerabilities?
- Are there processes in place to ensure that corrective actions, such as security updates or configuration changes, are implemented promptly?

This objective helps reduce the attack surface and vulnerability of information systems, improving the organization's overall cybersecurity.

<PracticeTitle name="threat-1a">Appropriate sources of vulnerability information have been identified.</PracticeTitle>

### Question

Has the organization identified suitable sources for obtaining information about vulnerabilities affecting its systems? Such sources may include software vendor security bulletins, security communities (such as CERT networks), or international security vulnerability databases. It's also important to assess whether these sources are actively used, even if the activity is not yet fully systematic.

### Significance

Identifying suitable vulnerability information sources is an essential part of the organization's ability to respond to cybersecurity risks. Keeping systems secure and up-to-date requires the organization to have access to current and reliable information about known vulnerabilities, such as security gaps in software or system configuration issues. This information helps the organization assess risks and take necessary actions to address vulnerabilities before threat actors can exploit them.

<AnswerOptions>
Evaluate to what extent the organization utilizes appropriate vulnerability information sources:

- **Not implemented or no information**: The organization has not identified vulnerability information sources or information on this matter is not available.
- **Partially implemented**: The organization has identified some vulnerability information sources, but their use is limited or irregular.
- **Mostly implemented**: Vulnerability information sources are widely used, but usage is not fully systematic or regular.
- **Fully implemented**: The organization comprehensively and regularly utilizes appropriate vulnerability information sources to identify vulnerabilities.
</AnswerOptions>

### Example

The company uses multiple information sources to identify vulnerabilities, such as software vendors' security bulletins, Finnish National Cyber Security Centre notifications, and international security community reports like CERT networks. These sources are utilized primarily when vulnerabilities are detected or when updates are installed. However, vulnerability information sources are not systematically monitored, but are consulted on an as-needed basis. In the future, the company aims to develop a more systematic approach where vulnerability information is collected and utilized more regularly and proactively.

<ExampleDocument title="Example document">
### Introduction

Food Company Ltd has identified several suitable information sources for vulnerability identification, but the utilization of these sources occurs on an ad hoc basis and is not yet systematic or regular at level 1. Vulnerability information is retrieved from sources as needed, especially when vulnerabilities are detected or when systems require updates.

### Suitable sources for vulnerability identification

The company has access to several different sources related to vulnerability identification, but the utilization of this information is not yet fully established or regular. Information sources are used especially to identify vulnerabilities in critical systems, such as ERP and SCADA systems, when deviations or vulnerabilities are detected.

- Software vendor security bulletins: Software vendors, such as ERP and SCADA system providers, publish information about known vulnerabilities and risks related to their software. The company utilizes this information especially in conjunction with system updates.
- Cyber Security Centre notifications: The company monitors notifications and alerts from the Finnish National Cyber Security Centre, which provide timely information about new cyber threats and known vulnerabilities. This information is used for vulnerability identification especially when it directly relates to the software or equipment used by the company.
- Security communities and expert networks: The company follows security community channels, such as CERT networks (Computer Emergency Response Team), which regularly publish reports on known vulnerabilities and security incidents. These sources are utilized when a new vulnerability is detected that requires more detailed analysis.
- International security vulnerability databases: The company also obtains vulnerability information from international databases, such as CVE (Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures) or NVD (National Vulnerability Database). These sources provide information about globally known vulnerabilities that may affect the systems used by the company.

### Case-by-case use of information sources

Information sources are used on an ad hoc basis, meaning information is sought and utilized when vulnerabilities are detected or when there is a need to assess a particular system's security status. Information collection and utilization are not systematic, nor are they checked at regular intervals.

- Case-by-case use: The company monitors information sources especially when suspicious activity or possible vulnerabilities are detected in information systems. This may occur, for example, when IT or OT systems exhibit abnormal behavior.
- No regular checking: Since vulnerability information sources are not monitored regularly, information is mainly sought when there is a specific need. This means that information is not checked proactively, but sources are relied upon when vulnerabilities are already detected.

### Diversity and utilization of information sources

Food Company utilizes several different sources that provide comprehensive information about various vulnerabilities, such as security gaps in software, hardware, or network equipment. These sources are used especially for vulnerability identification and risk assessment.

- Local and national information sources: The Finnish National Cyber Security Centre's bulletins and alerts are important especially for identifying nationally significant cybersecurity vulnerabilities. This information is used in situations where vulnerabilities may more broadly affect the entire industry or national infrastructure.
- International sources: International vulnerability information sources, such as software vendor and security organization reports, provide information especially about globally spreading vulnerabilities that may also affect the food company's systems. This information is used when vulnerabilities relate to international software or systems.
- Interpretation of vulnerability information: Collected vulnerability information is analyzed and interpreted as needed to determine how vulnerabilities affect the company's critical systems and processes. This helps the company make informed decisions to prevent possible vulnerabilities or reduce their impacts.

### Use of information in addressing vulnerabilities

Collected vulnerability information helps the company make decisions about necessary actions to address vulnerabilities or mitigate their impacts. These actions may be implemented, for example, by installing updates or changing system configurations to improve security.

- Installing updates: When information sources provide information about critical vulnerabilities, the company installs necessary updates or patches on a case-by-case basis. This helps ensure that vulnerabilities are addressed quickly and their impacts can be minimized.
- Configuration management: If vulnerability information indicates that certain system configurations may be vulnerable to exploitation, the company modifies these configurations. This may involve, for example, restricting access rights or changing network settings.
- Monitoring enhancement: If vulnerability information indicates an increased risk to certain systems, the company may increase monitoring of those systems. This may especially mean enhancing network monitoring or continuous surveillance of certain devices and software.

### Risks and challenges at level 1

Since vulnerability information collection and utilization are not systematic, there is a risk that certain vulnerabilities may not be detected in time or that vulnerability information becomes outdated before it is acted upon. This may lead to some vulnerabilities being realized without the company being adequately prepared for them.

- Information aging: Since information sources are not monitored regularly, it is possible that some vulnerability information becomes outdated before there is time to react to it. This may weaken the company's ability to address new or rapidly evolving vulnerabilities.
- Reactive approach: Vulnerability identification is currently based on a reactive operating model, which means that vulnerabilities are mainly reacted to when they have already been detected. The proactive approach is weaker because vulnerability information is not checked regularly.

### Development plan

Food Company Ltd recognizes the need to develop vulnerability information collection and utilization processes. The goal is to create a more systematic approach to collecting and sharing vulnerability information, so that all relevant vulnerabilities are detected and can be responded to in time.

- Regular vulnerability information monitoring: The company intends to implement a regular process for monitoring vulnerability information sources, so that information can be collected continuously and systematically. This helps improve the company's ability to identify new vulnerabilities and prepare for them proactively.
- Improving information utilization: Additionally, the company aims to improve vulnerability information analysis and use as part of risk management. This means that vulnerability information will be better integrated into the company's security practices and operational processes.
</ExampleDocument>

<PracticeTitle name="threat-1b">Information about vulnerabilities is collected and interpreted at least on an ad hoc basis. The activity does not need to be systematic and regular.</PracticeTitle>

### Question

Does the organization collect information about potential security vulnerabilities, such as software security gaps or network vulnerabilities, and examine it whenever a vulnerability or problem arises? This may mean that information is sought, for example, when suspicious activity is detected in systems or an external vulnerability is identified. Information is collected and analyzed on a case-by-case basis, not continuously or regularly.

### Significance

Collecting and interpreting vulnerability information are central to supporting the organization's cybersecurity. Information about vulnerabilities helps the company respond more quickly to possible security vulnerabilities and ensure that necessary actions can be implemented in a timely manner. Although the process is not fully systematic, it supports the organization in identifying and responding to vulnerabilities, especially in critical moments.

<AnswerOptions>
Evaluate how the organization collects and interprets vulnerability information:

- **Not implemented or no information**: Vulnerability information is not collected, and there is no practice for its interpretation.
- **Partially implemented**: Vulnerability information is collected, but the process is random and not analyzed regularly.
- **Mostly implemented**: Vulnerability information is collected from multiple sources, but analysis is not fully systematic and does not cover all necessary information sources.
- **Fully implemented**: Vulnerability information collection and interpretation are systematic and cover all key sources, and are actively utilized to support functions.
</AnswerOptions>

### Example

The company collects vulnerability information from various sources as needed, but the process is not yet systematic. Information is collected especially when vulnerabilities arise, such as in system updates or in connection with security vulnerabilities. Sources include among others the Finnish National Cyber Security Centre's alerts, software vendors' security bulletins, and international security portal reports. Information is interpreted on a case-by-case basis and used to support risk management.

Although the process is still reactive, the company intends to develop it to be more systematic and implement continuous vulnerability information monitoring, so that vulnerabilities can be identified proactively and responded to more quickly.

<ExampleDocument title="Example document">
### Introduction

Food Company Ltd collects and interprets vulnerability information to support its functions, but this occurs on an ad hoc basis and case-by-case. Vulnerability information collection and interpretation are not yet systematic or regular at level 1. ### Collecting vulnerability information on a case-by-case basis

The company collects vulnerability information from multiple sources always as needed. This information collection focuses especially on critical moments, such as in conjunction with system updates or when security vulnerabilities arise that may affect the company's operations.

- Cyber Security Centre alerts: The company monitors alerts and bulletins from the Finnish National Cyber Security Centre, from which it receives information about current cyber threats and known vulnerabilities. These sources are utilized on a case-by-case basis when a new vulnerability is detected, but they are not checked regularly.
- Software vendor security notifications: Software vendors', such as ERP and SCADA system providers', security bulletins are important information sources. These are monitored especially when software updates are made or when a specific vulnerability is detected in systems.
- Security experts and security portals: International security portals and security experts' blogs provide information more broadly about known vulnerabilities. These information sources are used when there is a need to check whether a detected vulnerability is related to a broader cyber attack or security issue.

### Interpreting vulnerability information for the function

When vulnerability information is collected, the company interprets it on a case-by-case basis based on how vulnerabilities affect the organization's operations. This interpretation is not fully regular, but is done especially when a new vulnerability is detected or when system security needs to be assessed.

- Case-by-case interpretation: Vulnerability information is analyzed based on what impact the vulnerability has on the company's key processes, such as production or security. Interpretation may include a risk assessment and a possible corrective plan to address the vulnerability.
- Using vulnerability information in decision-making: Vulnerability information interpretation helps the company make decisions about necessary actions. This may mean, for example, expediting a software update, increasing monitoring, or changing access rights for certain systems.

### Practical utilization of information sources and interpretation

Collected and interpreted vulnerability information is used for functions always as needed. Based on the information, the company can make corrective actions, such as installing security updates, changing system settings, or enhancing security practices.

- Installing updates: Interpreted vulnerability information often leads to software and system updates, especially when vulnerabilities have been detected or when software vendors recommend updates to be made for certain vulnerabilities.
- Increasing monitoring: If collected vulnerability information indicates deviations, the company may increase monitoring of certain critical systems, such as real-time network monitoring or closer monitoring of certain devices and servers.
- Modifying security practices: Based on vulnerability information, the company may also make changes to its security policy, such as restricting access rights or updating certain security practices.

### Case-by-case approach at level 1

Since vulnerability information collection and interpretation are not systematic or regular, they are relied upon mainly reactively. This means that the company acts according to how vulnerabilities appear from information sources, but does not anticipate vulnerabilities with regular checks or systematic analysis.

- No regular process: Vulnerability information collection and analysis are done always as needed, but information sources are not monitored continuously. This may mean that certain vulnerabilities go undetected if they are not visible or current at that particular moment.
- Reactive activity: Actions are taken when a new vulnerability or deviation is detected in the company. This may mean that vulnerability information is used only after a vulnerability or problem has already occurred.

### Risks and challenges at level 1

Since vulnerability information collection and interpretation occur on a case-by-case basis, there is a risk that some essential vulnerabilities go undetected or are not responded to quickly enough. This may weaken the company's preparedness to address new vulnerabilities or security attacks.

- Information aging: Since vulnerability information is not collected regularly, some vulnerability information may become outdated before the company has time to react to it. This may cause vulnerabilities that are not corrected in time.
- Reactive approach: Case-by-case activity means that the company reacts to vulnerabilities only after they are detected. The proactive approach, where vulnerabilities are sought to be identified in advance with regular information analysis, is weaker at level 1.

### Development plan

Food Company Ltd aims to develop vulnerability information collection and interpretation into a more systematic and regular process. The goal is to implement a proactive operating model where vulnerability information collection and analysis are a continuous part of risk management.

- Regular vulnerability information analysis: The company intends to implement a regular vulnerability information collection process where different information sources are monitored continuously. This helps the company detect vulnerabilities before they affect critical functions.
- Utilizing information proactively: The goal is also that collected vulnerability information is used proactively to address vulnerabilities, not just reactively. This means, for example, that security processes and systems are checked regularly based on vulnerability information.

<PracticeTitle name="threat-1c">Vulnerability assessments are performed at least on an ad hoc basis.</PracticeTitle>

### Question

Does the organization perform vulnerability assessments, at least when there is a specific need or when new vulnerabilities are detected? This may mean that vulnerability assessments are conducted in connection with system updates, software changes, or when security risks are identified. Vulnerability assessment helps identify potential vulnerabilities in systems and evaluate the need for corrective actions.

### Significance

Vulnerability assessments are a critical part of an organization's cybersecurity, as they help identify weaknesses in systems and processes that could be exploited by threat actors. Regular vulnerability assessment helps the organization understand where its systems are vulnerable and what measures are needed to reduce risks. Even if the activity is not yet fully systematic, conducting vulnerability assessments supports the organization in improving its security posture.

<AnswerOptions>
Evaluate how the organization performs vulnerability assessments:

- **Not implemented or no information**: Vulnerability assessments are not performed, or information about them is not available.
- **Partially implemented**: Vulnerability assessments are performed occasionally or on a case-by-case basis, but are not systematic or regular.
- **Mostly implemented**: Vulnerability assessments are performed quite regularly, but they do not cover all systems or are not fully systematic.
- **Fully implemented**: Vulnerability assessments are performed systematically and regularly for all critical systems, and their results are actively utilized to improve security.
</AnswerOptions>

### Example

The company performs vulnerability assessments on a case-by-case basis, especially when new systems are taken into use or when significant updates are made to existing systems. For example, vulnerability scans may be performed for network equipment and critical systems, such as ERP and SCADA systems, when changes are made. Assessments help identify potential vulnerabilities and take corrective actions, such as installing security updates or changing configurations. However, assessments are not yet systematic or regular, and they do not cover all systems continuously. In the future, the company aims to develop the process so that vulnerability assessments are performed more regularly and cover all critical systems comprehensively.

The company performs vulnerability assessments always when there is a specific need or when vulnerabilities in systems need to be assessed. The company uses various methods and tools to perform vulnerability assessments, but these are not yet fully standardized or systematically utilized. Assessments may include both automated tools and manual inspections.

- Automated vulnerability scanning: The company uses automated vulnerability scanning tools that help identify known vulnerabilities in systems and software. These tools are used especially when network equipment or critical systems, such as ERP or SCADA systems, need to be checked.

The results of vulnerability assessments are used to identify necessary corrective actions, such as security updates, configuration changes, or increased monitoring. However, assessments are not yet systematically documented or their results are not followed up on in the long term.

- Regular vulnerability assessments: The company intends to implement regular vulnerability assessments that cover all critical systems. This helps ensure that vulnerabilities are identified in time and corrective actions can be made proactively.
- Systematic documentation and tracking: The goal is also to ensure that all assessment results are documented and their impacts are tracked continuously. This improves the company's ability to analyze how effective the corrective actions have been and helps make necessary improvements.

<PracticeTitle name="threat-1d">Relevant vulnerabilities are addressed.</PracticeTitle>

### Question

Does the organization address relevant vulnerabilities when they are identified? This may mean, for example, installing security updates, changing system configurations, or increasing monitoring when vulnerabilities are detected. Are corrective actions taken quickly enough and is it ensured that vulnerabilities are addressed before they can be exploited?

### Significance

Addressing vulnerabilities is a critical part of an organization's cybersecurity. It's not enough to just identify vulnerabilities - they must also be effectively addressed to reduce risks and protect critical systems and data. Quick and effective addressing of vulnerabilities helps prevent cyber attacks and minimize possible damage.

<AnswerOptions>
Evaluate how the organization addresses relevant vulnerabilities:

- **Not implemented or no information**: Vulnerabilities are not addressed, or information about addressing them is not available.
- **Partially implemented**: Vulnerabilities are addressed on a case-by-case basis, but the process is not systematic or regular.
- **Mostly implemented**: Vulnerabilities are addressed quite systematically, but addressing may be delayed or may not cover all relevant vulnerabilities.
- **Fully implemented**: All relevant vulnerabilities are addressed systematically and quickly, and the effectiveness of corrective actions is monitored continuously.
</AnswerOptions>

### Example

The company addresses relevant vulnerabilities when they are identified, especially critical vulnerabilities that may affect production or information security. For example, if a software vendor publishes a critical security update, the company installs it as quickly as possible for affected systems. Less critical vulnerabilities may be addressed later, for example in conjunction with normal maintenance. However, the process is not yet fully systematic, and addressing may be delayed if resources are limited or if the vulnerability is assessed as low risk. In the future, the company aims to develop the process so that all relevant vulnerabilities are addressed more systematically and quickly, and the effectiveness of corrective actions is monitored better.

<ExampleDocument title="Example document">
### Introduction

Food Company Ltd addresses relevant vulnerabilities when they are identified, but this occurs on an ad hoc basis and is not yet systematic or regular at level 1. Relevant vulnerabilities are addressed especially when they are assessed as critical or when they may affect the company's key functions, such as production or information security.

### Addressing relevant vulnerabilities on a case-by-case basis

The company addresses vulnerabilities on a case-by-case basis, prioritizing especially critical vulnerabilities that may have significant impacts on business operations. Less critical vulnerabilities may be addressed later or in conjunction with normal system maintenance.

- Addressing critical vulnerabilities: If a vulnerability is assessed as critical, such as a security gap that could allow unauthorized access to systems or disrupt production, the company addresses it immediately. This may include installing security updates, changing configurations, or temporarily isolating systems.
- Addressing less critical vulnerabilities: Vulnerabilities that are assessed as lower risk may be addressed later, for example in conjunction with normal maintenance windows or system updates. This helps the company prioritize its resources and focus on the most important vulnerabilities first.

### Methods for addressing vulnerabilities

The company uses various methods to address vulnerabilities, depending on the nature and severity of the vulnerability. These methods include both technical solutions and organizational measures.

- Installing security updates: The most common way to address vulnerabilities is to install security updates or patches that software vendors have released. The company installs these updates especially for critical systems, such as ERP and SCADA systems, when critical vulnerabilities are detected.
- Configuration changes: If a vulnerability relates to system configuration, the company may change settings to reduce the risk. This may include, for example, restricting access rights, changing network settings, or strengthening authentication requirements.
- Temporary measures: In some cases, the company may implement temporary measures to address a vulnerability while a permanent solution is being prepared. This may include, for example, temporarily isolating a system from the network or increasing monitoring.

### Prioritizing vulnerabilities

The company prioritizes addressing vulnerabilities based on their criticality and possible impacts. Critical vulnerabilities that may affect business continuity or information security are addressed immediately, while less critical vulnerabilities may be addressed later.

- Criticality assessment: Each vulnerability is assessed based on its criticality and possible impacts. Factors taken into account in the assessment include the potential for exploitation, the impact on business operations, and the availability of corrective actions.
- Case-by-case prioritization: The company prioritizes addressing vulnerabilities on a case-by-case basis, taking into account current threats and available resources. This means that vulnerability situations are handled as they arise, but systematic and regular proactive vulnerability management is not yet in use at level 1.

### Documentation and tracking

Although the company addresses vulnerabilities on a case-by-case basis, documentation and tracking of corrective actions are not fully systematic at level 1. This may lead to a situation where some vulnerability-related corrective actions are not documented, which weakens long-term traceability and analysis.

- Lack of documentation: Since corrective actions are not always documented systematically, some actions taken to address vulnerabilities may not be recorded. This may make it difficult to evaluate the effectiveness of corrective actions retrospectively, especially if vulnerability tracking is not continuous.
- Tracking challenges: The impacts of addressing vulnerabilities are not tracked regularly, which may lead to some corrective actions not being sufficient or monitoring not continuing long enough. This may weaken the company's ability to manage vulnerabilities in the long term.

### Risks and challenges at level 1

Since addressing vulnerabilities is not systematic or regular, there is a risk that all relevant vulnerabilities are not handled in time. This may increase the company's exposure to cyber threats, especially when vulnerabilities evolve rapidly or are not detected early enough.

- Reactive activity: Addressing vulnerabilities is currently based on a reactive operating model, which means that corrective actions are made only after a vulnerability has already been detected. This may cause the company not to be fully prepared for new or rapidly evolving vulnerabilities.
- Delays in corrective actions: If vulnerabilities are not addressed immediately or systematically, they may develop into more serious problems before they are responded to. This may cause vulnerabilities that expose the company to greater security risks.

### Development plan

Food Company Ltd recognizes the need to improve the systematicity and regularity of addressing vulnerabilities. The company plans to develop processes whereby addressing vulnerabilities can be done more proactively and effectively, and all corrective actions are documented accurately.

- Systematic vulnerability management: The company intends to create a systematic approach where vulnerability information is monitored regularly and relevant vulnerabilities are addressed proactively. This helps prevent vulnerabilities from developing into more serious problems and improves the company's ability to respond quickly.
- Documentation and tracking of corrective actions: The goal is to ensure that all vulnerability-related corrective actions are documented and their impacts are monitored continuously. This improves the company's ability to analyze how effective the corrective actions have been and helps make necessary improvements.

<ObjectiveTitle name="threat-2">Threat defense and information sharing</ObjectiveTitle>

### Significance

This objective focuses on identifying and responding to cyber threats that may target the organization's operations. **Threats** are potential malicious activities or events that can harm the organization's information systems, data, or operations. Effective threat defense requires the organization to actively monitor threat information, identify potential threat actors and their objectives, and implement measures to defend against or mitigate threats.

Information sharing is also a key part of threat defense. The organization shares threat information both internally, between different departments and functions, and externally, such as with authorities, partners, or industry networks. This helps the organization stay aware of evolving threat landscapes and enables coordinated responses to threats.

### Answer instructions
